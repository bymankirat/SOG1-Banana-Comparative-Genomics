SOG1 in banana: the plant DNA-damage master switch, from one cloned gene to the whole family across land plants

SOG1 is the plant counterpart of p53: after DNA damage it is switched on by the ATM and ATR kinases and turns on DNA repair, stops the cell cycle or triggers cell death. In 2023 I worked on the banana gene Ma-SOG1 during research training at NABI, Mohali. This is the next phase, done entirely on public genomes: how many SOG1 genes banana has, where they sit in plant evolution, whether the phosphorylation switch and the DNA-binding domain are conserved, and which banana genes MaSOG1 is likely to control.

Phase 1 · NABI, Jul–Nov 2023 (lab)

  • Retrieved the Ma-SOG1 coding sequence from the banana genome and compared it with SOG1 from Arabidopsis, rice and moss
  • Conserved motifs (MEME), NAM domain (CD-Search) and a neighbour-joining tree of five proteins
  • Amplified Ma-SOG1 from banana leaf cDNA, cloned it into pJET1.2, confirmed it by Sanger sequencing, and moved it into the plant expression vector pRI101-AN for overexpression in Arabidopsis

    Methods

    • Data: proteomes and coding sequences from Ensembl Plants (release …), downloaded and fingerprinted (MD5) by the pipeline; Pfam NAM domain PF02365 from InterPro; AlphaFold DB models via UniProt (used only when the UniProt sequence is identical).
    • NAC family: longest protein per protein-coding gene; hmmsearch (pyhmmer) with Pfam gathering thresholds.
    • SOG1 clade: every NAC protein searched with phmmer against all Arabidopsis NACs; candidates are proteins of at least 250 residues with SOG1, NAC044 or NAC085 among their 8 best matches and within 70% of the best score. Proteins that match a clade gene strongly (E < 1e-30) but miss the Pfam cut-off are added. The final call comes from the tree: walking up from a gene, if the first group containing Arabidopsis genes holds only SOG1/NAC044/NAC085, the gene is in the clade; if it holds those plus their sister NACs, it is an ancestral copy. Checked against Ensembl Compara.
    • Tree: MAFFT L-INS-i, trimAl -automated1, IQ-TREE 2 with ModelFinder (BIC) and 1,000 ultrafast bootstraps.
    • SQ/TQ: sites in the tail (after the NAC domain) vs 10,000 composition-preserving shuffles; Benjamini–Hochberg across proteins.
    • Disorder: metapredict per residue, compared with AlphaFold pLDDT (Spearman).
    • Selection: codon alignments threaded onto the protein alignment; pairwise Nei–Gojobori dN/dS (Biopython) for domain and tail columns; pairs with dS outside 0.01–2 dropped.
    • Promoters: 1.5 kb upstream of each gene start (Ensembl REST); CTT(N)7AAG on both strands, counted over the whole 1.5 kb and within 500 bp of the gene start (window set in advance from SOG1 ChIP-seq); targets vs 1,000 random genes per species (fixed seed); banana orthologs by reciprocal best hit (E < 1e-10).
    • Reproducible: one command (python run_all.py), run end to end by a GitHub workflow; unit tests on known inputs and checks on the output.

    What this cannot tell you

    • Sequence and motif analyses predict function; they do not show it. Whether MaSOG1 rescues an Arabidopsis sog1 mutant is a lab question (phase 3).
    • A motif in a promoter is not binding: SOG1 ChIP data exist only for Arabidopsis.
    • Gene models come from each genome's annotation; a missed or split gene changes copy numbers.
    • Pairwise dN/dS averages over sites and lineages and pairs are not independent; it shows relative constraint, not positive selection.
