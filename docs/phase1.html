Ma-SOG1 in banana: from the genome to a plant expression vector

SOG1 is the plant counterpart of p53: when DNA breaks, the ATM and ATR kinases switch it on and it turns on DNA repair, stops the cell cycle or triggers cell death. During my training in the Plant Tissue Culture & Genetic Engineering lab at the National Agri-Food Biotechnology Institute, I found the banana copy of SOG1, analysed it on screen, cloned it from banana leaves and built a vector to overexpress it in Arabidopsis.

The banana SOG1 gene carries the NAC (NAM) DNA-binding domain and the SQ phosphorylation sites that ATM and ATR use to switch SOG1 on.

It groups with rice and Arabidopsis SOG1, apart from the two moss copies.

I amplified it from banana leaf cDNA and moved it into a plant expression vector ready for Agrobacterium-mediated transformation of Arabidopsis.

Why study SOG1 in banana?

Every cell has to repair its DNA. UV light, ionising radiation, reactive oxygen species and chemicals break or modify DNA, and the DNA damage response (DDR) senses the damage, signals it and repairs it. In plants the signal runs from the MRN complex to the ATM and ATR kinases, which phosphorylate SOG1 on serine–glutamine (SQ) sites in its tail. Active SOG1 then controls hundreds of genes for repair, cell-cycle arrest, cell death and endoreduplication.

SOG1 belongs to the large plant-specific NAC family of transcription factors (NAM, ATAF1/2, CUC2); two relatives, NAC044 and NAC085, also act in the DDR. Almost everything we know comes from Arabidopsis. Banana is a major crop with a genome shaped by repeated duplications, so its SOG1 may behave differently, and understanding it could help breed crops that tolerate stress better.

Aims

  1. Analyse the Ma-SOG1 gene sequence from the Banana Genome Hub in silico.
  2. Design primers to isolate and amplify Ma-SOG1.
  3. Clone Ma-SOG1 into the pJET1.2 cloning vector.
  4. Build the Ma-SOG1_pRI101-AN plant expression vector to overexpress Ma-SOG1 in Arabidopsis thaliana.

In silico analysis

Gene retrievedA single banana SOG1 candidate with a 1,251 bp coding sequence (416 amino acids), used for primer design.
NAC (NAM) domainCD-Search found the NAM superfamily domain in the N-terminal half, the part that binds DNA.
Conserved motifsMEME found five motifs; motifs 1–4 are shared by banana, rice, Arabidopsis and both moss SOG1 proteins, motif 5 only by the moss proteins.
SQ sitesThe alignment showed the SQ motifs in the C-terminal tail, the sites ATM and ATR phosphorylate to activate SOG1, in every sequence.

The Ma-SOG1 protein

416 residues: the NAC DNA-binding domain (residues 59–197) and a long flexible tail carrying five SQ sites (positions from the phase 2 analysis).

Evolutionary relationship

The neighbour-joining tree of five SOG1 proteins placed Ma-SOG1 with rice and Arabidopsis SOG1, and the two moss SOG1 proteins in a separate group. Phase 2 rebuilt this as a maximum-likelihood tree of 57 proteins from 17 genomes.

The plant expression construct

The confirmed insert was cut out with KpnI and SacI and ligated into the same sites of the binary vector pRI101-AN, placing Ma-SOG1 under a strong constitutive promoter for overexpression. Positive clones were confirmed by colony PCR, KpnI–SacI digestion (backbone plus insert) and Sanger sequencing, and the construct is ready for Agrobacterium-mediated transformation of Arabidopsis.

What came next

In phase 2 I followed the same gene across 17 plant genomes with public data. The CDS cloned here matches banana gene Ma03_g07280, the one-to-one ortholog of Arabidopsis SOG1. Banana has four SOG1-clade genes, the SQ switch is conserved, and the SOG1 binding site is enriched near banana DNA-repair genes.
